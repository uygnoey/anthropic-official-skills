# What teachers get

The capability side of the announcement — useful for framing a rollout to educators rather than to
administrators.

## Standards alignment

Teaching skills are **aligned with state academic standards across all 50 states**. A district can
therefore present adoption in terms of its own standards, which is a materially different
conversation from presenting a general-purpose assistant.

## The two new back-to-school skills

### Lesson Preparation

Assists educators in **planning key instructional moments**.

Applies to any subject. This is the lower-friction of the two for a first cohort, because it does
not depend on subject-specific coverage.

### Check for Understanding

Creates **standards-aligned assessments**, **initially available for mathematics**.

The mathematics-first availability is the main sequencing constraint in a rollout: a math department
can use both skills from day one, while other departments start with lesson preparation.

## Other improvements in the same update

- **Enhanced accessibility for student materials.**
- **Updated lesson planning functionality.**

## Using this in a rollout

Introduce the skills, not the product. Two concrete framings that follow directly from the above:

- *"Assessments that map to our state standards"* — Check for Understanding, math first.
- *"Help with the moments in a lesson that are hardest to plan"* — Lesson Preparation, any subject.

Pick the first cohort where the standards coverage is strongest. See the sibling
`compliance-and-eligibility.md` for the administrative side.
