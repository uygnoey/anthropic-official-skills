# Measuring an AI initiative, per Thomson Reuters

## The contrarian position

> "If you try to optimize too much for the rate of return calculation, you miss the forest for the
> trees." — Joel Hron

Hron prioritizes cultural mindset shifts before optimizing for cost-per-task metrics. The argument is
not that ROI is unmeasurable; it is that optimizing for it too early narrows what the organization
attempts.

## What is still tracked

- **Engineering measures** — DORA metrics and time-to-production.
- **Concrete task results** — an internal error-remediation tool built on Claude reduced root cause
  analysis from three hours to four minutes.

## The deeper change

The post's claim is that the more important shift is in the nature of the work rather than in its cost.

**For engineers:** "The act of writing lines of code is no longer the job." What matters now is systems
thinking, judgment, and taste.

**Across the organization:** the same pattern makes people "more T-shaped" — able to reach across
product, design, and finance rather than staying inside one function.

## How to use this when reporting

Report both layers, and do not let the first crowd out the second:

```
Task-level:      <cost per task, time saved, throughput — e.g. 3 hours → 4 minutes>
Engineering:     <DORA measures, time-to-production>
Capability:      <work now possible that was not before>
Mindset:         <where judgment, systems thinking, and taste have moved to the center of a role>
Reach:           <where people are working across functions they previously did not>
```

## What the post does not specify

- Which DORA measures moved, or by how much.
- How cultural mindset shifts are assessed.
- What ROI threshold, if any, a project has to meet.
