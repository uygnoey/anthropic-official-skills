# Enablement and governance components

The rollout described in the source article wrapped the firm-wide deployment in five
components: onboarding materials, training sessions, webinars, expert support, and
governance structures. User experience was then tracked through ongoing surveys and
feedback collection.

This file expands what each component covers and the order to build them in. The ordering
and the per-component checklists are a practical reading of the five named components; the
components themselves and the tracking mechanism are what the article reports.

## Build order

Ordered by what blocks a launch versus what scales one.

| Order | Component | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Governance structures | Blocks launch. Nothing ships until the rules exist. |
| 2 | Onboarding materials | Blocks launch. The default path for a new user on day one. |
| 3 | Training sessions | Scales launch. Depth for the populations that need it. |
| 4 | Webinars | Scales launch. Breadth, and a recurring cadence after go-live. |
| 5 | Expert support | Catches what 1–4 miss, and tells you what to add to them. |
| — | Surveys and feedback | Runs continuously from the pilot onward. |

## Governance structures

The prerequisite. Decide and write down, before any general availability date:

- Which data may be used with which surface, stated per surface rather than once globally.
- What is approved for client-facing work versus internal work only.
- Who owns the decision when a new surface or integration is proposed.
- What happens to output that will be delivered to a client — review, attribution, retention.
- The escalation path when someone is unsure.

Governance written after launch is governance written in response to an incident.

## Onboarding materials

The self-serve path a person follows on their first day of access. It should be possible to
complete without booking time with anyone.

- One page per surface, not one page for the deployment.
- First-task guidance specific to the role, not a feature tour.
- The governance rules restated in the place where they bind, rather than only in a policy doc.
- A pointer to expert support for the case the page does not cover.

## Training sessions

Live, role-specific, and hands-on. This is where the surface that sits inside a role's daily
tool gets its uptake — the reported adoption of Claude for Excel by more than two-thirds of
pilot participants is the kind of number a role-specific session moves.

- Segment by role, not by seniority.
- Use the organization's own work as the exercise material.
- Run them again for each later cohort; a single wave of training decays.

## Webinars

Breadth and cadence. Cheaper per head than training, and useful for the population that
needs awareness rather than skill.

- One at launch, then a recurring series.
- Record them; the recording becomes onboarding material for the next cohort.
- Use them to broadcast what expert support is seeing repeatedly.

## Expert support

A staffed channel where people bring the case the materials did not cover. Its second job is
diagnostic: the questions arriving repeatedly are the gaps in onboarding and training.

- Route by surface so the answer comes from someone who uses that surface.
- Feed recurring questions back into the onboarding pages within the week.

## Surveys and feedback

Ongoing, not one-shot. A launch survey measures the launch. A recurring one measures whether
adoption is holding.

Worth tracking:

- Active users per surface, per week — the pilot metric, kept running.
- Which surface each population actually settles on.
- Where people stopped, and why.
- Whether governance rules are understood or merely published.
