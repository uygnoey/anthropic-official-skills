# Kickoff and delegation prompts

Every prompt below is taken from the post. Use them verbatim or adapt the shape.

## Starting from an idea you have not formed yet

**Ask Claude to interview you**

> Before you start, ask me everything you need to know to get this right.

**Ask for directions**

> Here is roughly what I want. Give me three ways you could take it, with a quick sample of each.

**React to something external, with your own context in play**

> Here's the announcement, tell me what changes for us, considering everything you know about my
> organization.

Keep the task in the same conversation as the brainstorm. Claude Fable 5 then already carries the goal
you settled on, the constraints you named, and the decisions you made along the way.

## Delegating a whole job

**Delegate the approach** — give the material and the outcome, not the method:

> Here is last quarter's customer feedback. Find out why cancellations rose and what we should change.

**Delegate the procedure** — let Claude pick the skills:

> Put together the quarterly review the way we always do it.

**Delegate the timing** — describe the outcome and let Claude build the schedule:

> I want to start every Monday knowing what changed in the pipeline and what needs a decision.

## Briefing shape

Brief the way you would brief a colleague on a report — purpose, timeline, and what it has to
accomplish — rather than a list of rules. A constraint like *"keep it under two pages and use plain
language"* is fine and still useful, but it only tells Claude what not to do. Context tells it what
the work is for, so it can make the right call in situations your constraints did not anticipate.

To give Claude something concrete to check itself against, share a pair:

> Here is an early draft of a report and here is the final version. Work out what my standards are
> from what changed between the two, then apply them to this new one.

## Reviewing and auditing

**Trace a number back to its source**

> Where did this figure come from?

**Set a standing voice preference** (prompt or project instructions)

> Use plain, straightforward, direct language.

**Audit instructions written for an older model**

> Go through my skills and saved memory. Which still fit, and which were written for an older model?
