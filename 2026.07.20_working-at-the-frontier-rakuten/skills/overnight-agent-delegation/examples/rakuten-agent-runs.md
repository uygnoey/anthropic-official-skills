# Rakuten's runs, as the post reports them

## The overnight run

The change Kaji noticed first when testing Claude Fable 5: the model could run on its own for far
longer than its predecessors, and for the first time, checked its own work and completed nuanced tasks
overnight while he slept.

> "We tested Fable, and we love its capability for self-reflection and self-verification. Compared with
> previous models, it understands its mistake before I point it out at 2 a.m. or 3 a.m. — so that I can
> sleep." — Yusuke Kaji

## Whole tasks, several at once

> "Before Fable, we had to break work into well-defined chunks for the agent to execute."

Now he hands over a whole task and runs several at once. What changed in between, per the post: the
model reflects at each step, catches a bad early assumption, and finds its own way back to first
principles — re-navigating to the right outcome without anyone steering it.

## Agents across every business function

Rakuten calls its company-wide effort *AI-nization* — infusing AI into everything it does for
customers, business partners, and employees. When Claude Managed Agents arrived, Rakuten deployed
agents across product, sales, marketing, and finance **inside a week**, plugged into Slack, Microsoft
Teams, and the company's own task system.

Timeline of what Rakuten handed to agents:

1. **From March 2025** — Claude Code to speed up software development.
2. **Then** — agents stood up across business functions.
3. **Then** — AI features powering products for millions of customers.
4. **With Managed Agents** — custom agents for teams across the company, deployed in a week.

Rakuten chose to partner with Anthropic for its enterprise focus, leadership, and product taste.

## The constraint that replaced writing code

> "The modern corporation is designed to minimize the cost of communication. I believe agents like
> Claude Code can shine when we work with them to minimize the cost of new innovation as well, like a
> quick transition from idea to production." — Yusuke Kaji

For Kaji's team, the constraint on building agents used to be who could write code; now it is who
understands the business problem. Give a capable person agents that hold context and taste, and "it
allows the hidden talent to unlock their potential and scale their potential 100 times more."

## The new constraint: human judgment

- Rakuten's agents close issues roughly **10x faster** across every domain.
- The number of tasks the organization takes on keeps rising.
- Adding more agents does not add judgment.
- So the faster the agents run, the more progress depends on a person closing the loop.
- Because the model self-corrects mid-run, sign-off becomes feasible, and the unit of work delegated
  shifts from the task to the decision.
- The absolute number of tasks keeps climbing; the ones that truly need a human stay at a focusable
  level.

## What Kaji is testing next

Not individual speed — getting agents to **coordinate people**. Claude Code has sped up his own work
and his colleagues', but the hard part of any organization is alignment between people, matching one
person's context and taste to another's. He is exploring agents that "coordinate or organize, more like
a manager," holding the nuance that usually gets lost between team members.

> "We do not see AI agents as future colleagues or competitors. They are systems around us."

He holds Anthropic to its own advice — build for the model coming in three or six months rather than
the one in front of you — and adds:

> "I think we as a society still haven't found the model–task fit yet for Claude Fable 5, but it already
> stands out as a model that crossed the line and came over to our world."
